# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

Everyone who uses, contributes to, or discusses SafeSurf should feel welcome, whether they found a bug in the URL parser, maintain a service that depends on this library, or are reading the source for the first time. We commit to keeping the project's spaces free of harassment and personal attacks, regardless of a person's background, identity, level of experience, or nationality.

## Expected behavior

- Write clearly and assume good intent. Many contributors work in a second language.
- Critique code, not people. "This check produces a false positive" is useful; "you broke it" is not.
- Stay on topic in issues and pull requests, or start a new one.
- Phishing and abuse are the subject of this library, not acceptable behavior in its community. Do not post links intended to harm readers, and do not use SafeSurf's source to build scam tooling.
- When you test against real URLs, follow the guidance in CONTRIBUTING.md: never submit live phishing sites to the test suite.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, insults, or discriminatory language directed at anyone.
- Publishing other people's private information without their consent.
- Deliberate trolling, bad-faith arguments, or repeatedly derailing discussions after a maintainer has asked you to stop.
- Posting malware samples or live phishing URLs in plain text. If a sample is genuinely needed for a report, describe it or use a defanged form.
- Any attempt to use project communication channels for spam, scam recruitment, or driving traffic to malicious sites.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other channel representing the SafeSurf community. It also applies when you represent the project publicly, for example at a conference or in another repository.

## Enforcement

Report violations by email to [dika@kua.lat](mailto:dika@kua.lat). Include links to the messages involved and any context that helps. Reports are handled by the project maintainer and kept confidential to the extent possible.

The maintainer will respond to acknowledge the report, then investigate and decide on a proportionate response. Possible actions range from a private warning to removing comments, blocking a participant, or banning them from project spaces. Repeated or severe violations lead to a permanent ban. Everyone, including the maintainer, is expected to follow this document.

If you believe an enforcement decision was wrong, reply to the response you received and the decision will be reviewed once more.
